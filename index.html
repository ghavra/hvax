The Problem

Bid windows are short.
Document sets aren't.

Commercial electrical estimators work under real pressure. Thorough document review competes with takeoff and pricing time — and review almost always loses.

01

Volume you can't read end-to-end

A single commercial bid can span hundreds of pages across drawings, specs, addenda, alternates, and supplemental documents. Reading everything is not a realistic option.

02

Contradictions across documents

Specs say one thing. Drawings say another. Addenda change something that wasn't updated elsewhere. These contradictions surface as disputes — or worse, unbilled work — after award.

03

Scope that isn't clearly defined

Vague interface language, undefined responsibilities between divisions, and unlisted equipment leave estimators guessing — and often underbidding work they'll be held to.

04

Late addenda buried in the stack

Addenda arrive after most of the estimate is built. A missed panel schedule revision or scope clarification can shift the job significantly and go unpriced.

05

Multiple bids, compressed timelines

Estimators are managing several active bids simultaneously. There's no slack in the schedule for a second, methodical pass through every document in a package.

06

One miss becomes a margin problem

A single missed requirement — a fire alarm interface, a temporary power obligation, an undefined commissioning scope — can cost more to address post-award than it would have cost to price correctly.

What BidEyes looks for

Six categories of issues that most often create bid risk, scope disputes, or unbilled work on commercial electrical projects.

⚠️

Bid Risks

Liquidated damages, broad indemnification, undefined scope ownership, or one-sided responsibility transfer — conditions that create financial exposure before you've even started the job.

🔀

Contradictions

Conflicts between specifications, drawings, addenda, and the ITB — wire sizes, equipment ratings, installation methods, or scope boundaries that don't agree across documents.

📭

Missing Requirements

Referenced work that isn't fully specified: commissioning, spare parts, submittals, testing and inspection, temporary power, startup services — scope that exists in the documents but not in the spec.

🕳

Scope Gaps

Interface work between divisions, unassigned coordination responsibilities, and equipment furnished by others with no installation scope defined — the grey areas estimators have to guess at.

🔍

Items to Investigate

Ambiguities, major addenda changes, complex notes, or unusual material requirements that warrant an estimator's direct attention, judgment, and manual review.

📌

Potential RFIs

Design gaps, unresolved coordination issues, and ambiguities where a clarification before submission reduces your risk — identified and ready to issue before the bid goes out.

How BidEyes works

Pre-bid risk review for commercial electrical subcontractors.

You send the bid package. Our pipeline reads it, compares the documents against each other, and returns findings an estimator can check in minutes, each with a citation to the exact page.

IN

ITBs, drawings, specifications, addenda, alternates and the bid form.

OUT

A pre-bid control sheet (priority, issue, source, bid impact, action, owner, status), an addenda delta page, RFI-ready questions, and a citation on every finding. For example: a mismatch between a drawing and a spec section, with the sheet, the section, and the exact wording from each.

Step by step

1. Read and index (our code)

Every page of your package becomes a record that carries its document, page number, sheet number and spec section. Scanned pages are read with OCR. Any page we cannot read is listed in your report so you know what was not covered. Your report states how many pages were reviewed.

2. Map what changed (our code)

Late addenda are where bids go wrong. We build a change map: which sheets were reissued, which spec sections an addendum replaced, added or deleted, and which alternates or bid-form lines changed. We also sort pages by topic so each request to Claude contains only what it needs.

3. Claude reads across documents (Claude API from Anthropic)

BidEyes uses the Claude API from Anthropic to extract evidence and draft findings. For each topic, drawing sheets, spec sections and the relevant addenda are placed in the same request, so Claude can read across them and compare what each one says. Topics include scope boundaries, drawing versus spec conflicts, addenda impact, long-lead equipment, contract terms, schedule and phasing, alternates, and testing requirements. Claude must return every finding with an exact quote and a page reference. It is not allowed to return a finding without evidence.

4. Check the evidence (our code and Claude)

Our code checks that every quote appears word for word on the page it cites. Claims that something is missing from scope are searched for across the whole package, and dropped if the item is covered elsewhere. A second request then tries to disprove each remaining finding, including checking whether a later addendum already resolved it. Anything that fails is discarded, and the reason is logged.

5. Estimator review (people)

The founder reviews every high-priority finding before the report is delivered. You receive a short list, with borderline items kept separate. Your estimator checks each citation and makes the final call.

What Claude does and does not do

Claude assists with evidence extraction and drafting. It does not replace estimating judgment, code review, or contractual advice.

BidEyes can miss items and can flag things that turn out not to be issues. That is why every finding carries a citation and is reviewed by a person before it reaches you.

Flags potential risks for estimator review. It does not guarantee that no scope is missed.
