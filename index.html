The Problem

Bid windows are short.
Document sets aren't.

Commercial electrical estimators work under real pressure. Thorough document review competes with takeoff and pricing time — and review almost always loses.

01

Volume you can't read end-to-end

A single commercial bid can span hundreds of pages across drawings, specs, addenda, alternates, and supplemental documents. Reading everything is not a realistic option.

02

Contradictions across documents

Specs say one thing. Drawings say another. Addenda change something that wasn't updated elsewhere. These contradictions surface as disputes — or worse, unbilled work — after award.

03

Scope that isn't clearly defined

Vague interface language, undefined responsibilities between divisions, and unlisted equipment leave estimators guessing — and often underbidding work they'll be held to.

04

Late addenda buried in the stack

Addenda arrive after most of the estimate is built. A missed panel schedule revision or scope clarification can shift the job significantly and go unpriced.

05

Multiple bids, compressed timelines

Estimators are managing several active bids simultaneously. There's no slack in the schedule for a second, methodical pass through every document in a package.

06

One miss becomes a margin problem

A single missed requirement — a fire alarm interface, a temporary power obligation, an undefined commissioning scope — can cost more to address post-award than it would have cost to price correctly.

What BidEyes looks for

Six categories of issues that most often create bid risk, scope disputes, or unbilled work on commercial electrical projects.

⚠️

Bid Risks

Liquidated damages, broad indemnification, undefined scope ownership, or one-sided responsibility transfer — conditions that create financial exposure before you've even started the job.

🔀

Contradictions

Conflicts between specifications, drawings, addenda, and the ITB — wire sizes, equipment ratings, installation methods, or scope boundaries that don't agree across documents.

📭

Missing Requirements

Referenced work that isn't fully specified: commissioning, spare parts, submittals, testing and inspection, temporary power, startup services — scope that exists in the documents but not in the spec.

🕳

Scope Gaps

Interface work between divisions, unassigned coordination responsibilities, and equipment furnished by others with no installation scope defined — the grey areas estimators have to guess at.

🔍

Items to Investigate

Ambiguities, major addenda changes, complex notes, or unusual material requirements that warrant an estimator's direct attention, judgment, and manual review.

📌

Potential RFIs

Design gaps, unresolved coordination issues, and ambiguities where a clarification before submission reduces your risk — identified and ready to issue before the bid goes out.

Frequently asked questions

How does a subcontractor buy, pilot or test BidEyes?
We are currently operating in a Private Beta. You can request a pilot, and we will run your active or historical bids through our pipeline to surface risks you may have missed. Pilot pricing is custom-tailored to your estimating volume.
What file types can I upload?
Standard PDFs. We process Instructions to Bidders (ITBs), Project Manuals (specifications), drawing sets, and addenda exactly as you receive them.
Is my bid data used to train AI?
No. We use commercial API agreements that strictly prohibit training foundational models on your documents. Your data is your intellectual property. Read our full data commitments on our Security page.
How long do you keep my documents?
By default, we permanently purge all project files 30 days after your final report is delivered, or immediately upon your request.
Does it replace my estimator?
Absolutely not. BidEyes gives your estimating team a documented second set of eyes before the bid goes out. It does not replace estimator judgment, takeoff, or the Architect/Engineer's answer to an RFI. It simply surfaces the commercial and scope conflicts your estimator should know about before the number is set.
How accurate is it / what are the limits?
It does not promise to find everything. Where the documents are silent, the finding says so and does not guess. Because it can miss items, every single finding we deliver includes the exact source quote, its impact, and a specific action so your team can quickly verify the risk themselves.
How long does a review take?
Pilot reviews are delivered 38 to 48 hours before your bid is due, giving your team plenty of time to review the findings, adjust your number, or submit RFIs.
